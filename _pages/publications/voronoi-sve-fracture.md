Fracture patterns in heterogeneous materials depend strongly on random variations in the microstructure, where cracks nucleate and propagate. Accurate fracture simulation therefore needs material property data at intermediate scales, between the scale of the heterogeneity and that of a representative volume element (RVE). Statistical volume elements (SVEs), obtained by partitioning an RVE, give a probabilistic description of material properties at this mesoscale.

Defining an SVE requires modeling choices, including how the domain is partitioned, the element size, and the loading condition used to estimate its apparent behavior. This paper develops a Voronoi tessellation based partitioning scheme and applies it at several length scales to heterogeneous materials with various contrast ratios.

**Key findings**

- The failure strength of a given SVE varies with the direction of loading.
- SVE properties depend on the boundary condition applied, so the loading choice is an important modeling assumption.
- The mesoscale property statistics are used as input to a fracture model based on the asynchronous spacetime discontinuous Galerkin finite element method.
