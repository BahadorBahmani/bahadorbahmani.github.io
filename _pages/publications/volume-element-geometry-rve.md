Simulating fracture accurately requires accounting for small-scale randomness in material properties. Below the size of a representative volume element (RVE), statistical volume elements (SVEs) are used, and their apparent properties are random rather than uniquely defined. Both the constitutive behavior and the strength of SVEs must therefore be described statistically.

This paper examines how the geometry used to partition a material into SVEs affects the resulting distributions of mesoscale properties. It applies a Voronoi tessellation based partitioning scheme and compares the resulting property distributions with those from SVEs generated by square partitioning. SVE strength is approximated by the proportional limit stress, and failure strength distributions for loading at different angles are obtained by superposing elastic solutions.

**Key findings**

- The choice of partitioning geometry can strongly influence the probability distributions of mesoscale material properties.
- Property distributions from Voronoi-based SVEs are compared directly with those from square SVEs.
- Superposition of elastic results provides strength distributions for loading at variable angles.
